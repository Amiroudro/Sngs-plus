# Editorial and source-handling policy

## Source integrity
- Keep the publisher, author where available, original headline, publication/update timestamps, and canonical source URL.
- Link readers to the original reporting. Do not present publisher text as our own.
- Do not bypass paywalls, logins, technical restrictions, or other access controls.
- Check publisher terms and robots.txt before building automated collection. Prefer official feeds and permitted public metadata.
- RSS may provide only an excerpt. A missing full text is not permission to defeat access restrictions.

## AI output
- Summaries must be newly written and materially shorter than the source.
- Clearly separate reported facts, background context, and AI explanation.
- Never fabricate quotations, figures, dates, names, or source corroboration.
- Preserve uncertainty, disagreement, corrections, and attribution.
- A cluster of similar headlines is not proof of independent confirmation. Track which publishers independently report a fact.
- Keep source text and generated outputs traceable so errors can be corrected.

## Importance
Assess public impact, national or international significance, novelty, policy/economic implications, and exam relevance. Do not equate popularity with importance. An urgent public-safety event may require an editorial override.

## Bilingual quality
English and Bangla versions must preserve the same underlying facts, dates, quantities, names, and uncertainty. Use natural Bangla rather than literal word-for-word translation.

## Human review
High-impact, sensitive, disputed, or fast-changing stories should be flagged for human review before prominent publication.
